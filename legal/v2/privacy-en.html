Preamble

Athlomed is a condition and injury management platform for sports settings, built on the principle of handling data that may constitute medical information to the minimum extent necessary, while providing the recording functions that sports settings actually need.

This Policy explains how that principle is implemented in practice. Rather than stating abstract commitments, it sets out what we collect, who can see it, who cannot, where it is stored, and when it is erased.

Article 2 (Definitions)

  1. "Personal information" means information under Article 2(1) of Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information (the "APPI").
  2. "Special care-required personal information" means information under Article 2(3) of the APPI. In the Service, injury history, diagnoses, injury mechanism, whether surgery was performed, images of the affected area, and findings recorded in clinical notes are handled as potentially falling within this category.
  3. Other terms have the meanings given in Article 2 of the Terms of Service.

Article 3 (Our Role in the Service)

This Article is the foundation of this Policy.

  1. In respect of health records concerning athletes, the personal information handling business operator (controller) is, as a rule, the Contracting User (Medical user) who created the record. We hold and process those records as an entrusted party (processor) acting for that Contracting User.

We take this position because it is the Contracting User who exercises and records clinical judgement, and we are not in a position to judge that content.

  1. Notwithstanding the preceding paragraph, we are the controller in respect of:
    • account information (name, email address, role, display language);
    • contract and payment information;
    • access logs, audit logs and incident response records;
    • records of enquiries.
  2. Where an athlete has created an account but is not connected to any Team, we hold the records they enter on their own behalf. During that period, only the athlete can view them.
  3. Contracting Users bear the obligations that follow from being a controller — specifying and notifying purposes of use, security control measures, responding to data subject requests, and reporting breaches. As processor, we support the technical performance of those obligations.

Article 5 (Special Care-Required Personal Information)

  1. Collection of special care-required personal information requires the prior consent of the data subject (or, for those under 18, their legal representative).
  2. In the Service, that consent is obtained on the screen where the athlete issues a Connection Code. What is consented to is disclosure, to that Team, of records within the selected history scope.
  3. We record, as evidence of consent:
    • the type of consent;
    • the language displayed at the moment of consent;
    • the version of the consent text;
    • the date, time and identity of the person consenting;
    • the Team connected and the history scope selected.
  4. We record the language of consent so that "what was consented to" remains identifiable even if the display language is later changed. We consider that consent obtained by displaying Japanese text to an athlete who does not read Japanese is not valid consent to the collection of special care-required personal information.
  5. Consent may be withdrawn at any time, by releasing the connection or narrowing the history scope. No detriment follows from withdrawal. Information already viewed cannot be retrospectively unseen.

Article 6 (Purposes of Use)

We use the information we collect only for the following purposes:

  1. providing, maintaining and operating the Service;
  2. identity verification, authentication and account management;
  3. concluding contracts, billing and payment;
  4. recording, displaying, aggregating and visualising condition, injury, rehabilitation and return data;
  5. delivering reminders and alerts;
  6. responding to enquiries;
  7. detecting, preventing and responding to misuse;
  8. investigating and recovering from incidents;
  9. complying with law;
  10. producing statistics and improving the Service, after processing so that individuals cannot be identified (only where the data subject has given optional consent).

We do not use the information for advertising or promotion, and we do not sell it to third parties.

7.2 Notes

  1. "Within scope" means the history scope selected by the athlete at connection (from the join date only / including injury history / full history). The athlete may change it at any time.
  2. Staff of other Teams cannot view the data of a Team to which they are not connected. This restriction is implemented not only in the interface but by row-level access control in the database and by server-side permission checks.
  3. Staff belonging to several Teams can view the data of only one Team at a time. Switching requires the password to be re-entered and is recorded in the audit log.
  4. The reason athletes cannot view clinical note bodies is set out in Article 19 of the Terms of Service. Disclosure may be sought by a request under Article 13 of this Policy.
  5. Records are not disclosed to guardians through the Service interface. Guardians confirm content through a request under Article 13 or through the Team's Medical user.

Article 8 (Provision to Third Parties)

  1. We do not provide personal information to third parties except:
    • with the consent of the data subject;
    • as required by law;
    • where necessary to protect life, body or property and consent is difficult to obtain;
    • where cooperation with a national body is required and obtaining consent would impede the performance of that body's duties.
  2. Viewing of records by staff of a connected Team within the Service does not constitute provision to a third party under this Article (it is use within the purpose and entrusted processing).
  3. Where records are provided to a medical institution or other third party, separate consent is obtained for each provision. The procedure and form are set out in the Third-Party Disclosure Consent third_party_disclosure_consent.
  4. Where a provision is made, we and the Contracting User making it create and retain records of the provision in accordance with law.

Article 10 (Provision to Third Parties Abroad and Awareness of the External Environment)

  1. In connection with the subcontracting above, personal data may be handled outside Japan (currently, in the United States).
  2. The United States does not have a personal information protection regime recognised as equivalent to Japan's. We require our subcontractors by contract to implement measures equivalent to the security control measures required under the APPI.
  3. We maintain awareness of the personal information protection regimes of the countries in which our subcontractors are located and review them periodically.
  4. Data subjects may request information about provision to third parties abroad through the contact point in Article 17.

  1. Where an athlete releases a connection, or a Contracting User removes an athlete from a Team, the records themselves are not deleted. They remain attached to the Athlete Profile, and the athlete may continue to view and export them.
  2. Where an athlete withdraws, we delete the Athlete Profile and the records the athlete entered. Clinical notes created by a Contracting User are handled under Article 13(4).
  3. Records in the Service — whether entered by a Contracting User or by the athlete — are stored against the Athlete Profile. Release of the connection on cancellation means the records cease to be visible to that Contracting User; it does not mean the records are erased.
  4. After release, if the athlete issues a new Connection Code, staff of the new Team can view the records within the history scope the athlete selects.
  5. What the athlete may view and export is the records the athlete entered themselves. Records created by a Contracting User (clinical notes, injuries, measurements) are not shown on the athlete's screens, as set out in Article 7.

Article 13 (Requests for Disclosure, Correction and Suspension)

  1. Data subjects (or, for those under 18, their legal representative) may request notification of the purpose of use, disclosure, correction, addition, deletion, suspension of use, erasure and suspension of provision to third parties.
  2. The first point of contact is, as a rule, the Contracting User who created the record (Article 3(1)).
  3. Where that Contracting User cannot be reached, or where the data subject prefers to contact us directly, we receive the request at the contact point in Article 17. For information as to which we are the processor, we refer the request to the Contracting User and act on their instructions.
  4. For records that have the character of clinical records, we respond, as a rule, by logical deletion and suspension of access rather than physical deletion, balancing the need to retain records against the rights of the data subject.
  5. We require information sufficient to verify identity.
  6. We do not charge a fee for disclosure requests.
  7. We may be unable to meet a request in whole or in part where:
    • doing so would risk harm to the life, body, property or other rights and interests of the data subject or a third party;
    • doing so would significantly impede the proper conduct of our business;
    • doing so would breach law.

In such cases we notify the reason.

Note: The treatment in paragraph 4 remains under review by legal counsel.

Article 14 (Persons Under 16 and Minors)

  1. Where a person under 18 uses the Service, the consent of a guardian (legal representative) is required, obtained under the Guardian Consent guardian_consent.
  2. We apply a stricter standard than the statutory benchmark (broadly, under 18): guardian (legal representative) consent is required for everyone under 18, because the Service handles injury and rehabilitation records and other special care-required personal information on an ongoing basis.
  3. Guardians may make requests under Article 13 in respect of their child's records, through the contact point in Article 17.
  4. The Service does not provide guardians with a function to view records directly. This follows from the design principle that the athlete decides who may see their records. Guardians wishing to confirm content should do so through a request under Article 13 or through the Team's Medical user.
Note: The method of obtaining consent from minors and the extent of guardian involvement remain under review by legal counsel. This Article and the Guardian Consent may be revised as a result.

Article 15 (Withdrawal of Consent)

  1. Data subjects may withdraw consent at any time.
  2. Methods of withdrawal:

| Subject | Method |
|---|---|
| Disclosure to a particular Team | Release the connection, or narrow the history scope |
| Statistics and improvement | Withdraw individually in settings |
| Reminders | Withdraw individually in settings |
| Use of the Service generally | Withdraw from the Service |

  1. No detriment follows from withdrawal of consent.
  2. Processing already carried out at the time of withdrawal — information already viewed, information already provided — cannot be retrospectively undone.

Article 16 (Cookies)

  1. The Service uses cookies to maintain login state. These are necessary to provide the Service; if disabled, the Service cannot be used.
  2. The Service does not use advertising cookies or third-party behavioural tracking.
  3. The Service may use browser storage to retain display language and display preferences.
